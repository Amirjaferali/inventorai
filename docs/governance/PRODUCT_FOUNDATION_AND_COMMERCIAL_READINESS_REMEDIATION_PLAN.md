## 3. Owner Product Direction

The following owner direction must be converted into formal Owner Decisions before implementation.

### 3.1 Product name and branding

1. `InventorAI` is a temporary working name.
2. The final public name remains undecided because of possible market and intellectual-property conflicts.
3. The product must support centralized brand replacement.
4. Product identity must not be hard-coded into core contracts, database meaning, domain logic, or engine behavior.
5. Future branding must use centrally managed values such as `PRODUCT_NAME`, `PRODUCT_SHORT_NAME`, `PRODUCT_TAGLINE`, `LEGAL_ENTITY_NAME`, `SUPPORT_EMAIL`, and `PRIMARY_DOMAIN`.
6. Renaming must not alter project IDs, evidence IDs, API resource identities, or historical audit records without an explicit migration decision.

### 3.2 Multi-domain product identity

1. Electrical and Electronics were selected only as the initial experimental MVP scope.
2. They are not the permanent product boundary.
3. The long-term product must support multiple technology domains and cross-domain inventions.
4. Future technologies must be addable without rebuilding or hard-coding the core engine.
5. One project may belong to multiple domains and contain multiple subsystems.
6. Unsupported or inactive domains must not be presented as currently available.

### 3.3 Priority future domains and capabilities

Priority future areas include IoT, drone and unmanned systems, renewable energy, mechanical systems, robotics, software, medical devices, energy storage, manufacturing technologies, environmental technologies, and additional future technologies authorized later.

This is an extensible list, not a closed taxonomy.

### 3.4 IoT

IoT is both a selectable future technology domain and a cross-domain capability.

It may span electronics, sensors, connectivity, embedded software, edge processing, cloud and data, power, cybersecurity, device identity, firmware, telemetry, updates, device management, maintenance, and privacy.

IoT must not be implemented as a single shallow category or as hard-coded core branching.

### 3.5 Evidence, provenance, contribution, and ownership claims

The future product must include an:

`Evidence, Provenance, Contribution and Ownership-Claims Register`

The register must eventually support evidence items, source attribution, timestamps, contributor records, authorship claims, ownership claims, AI-generated versus user-supplied content, expert-supplied content, external-source content, decision linkage, gap linkage, question linkage, version history, file hashes, audit history, conflicting evidence, review status, confidence and verification status, exportable evidence packages, access control, and retention/deletion rules.

The application may document claims and evidence but MUST NOT claim to legally determine ownership, inventorship, patentability, freedom to operate, prior-art clearance, legal validity, filing readiness, or entitlement.

### 3.6 Commercial product

The future product is intended to support paid subscription plans.

Required future capabilities include accounts, profile, settings, help, real logout, authentication, authorization, durable persistence, subscriptions, billing, payments, entitlements, invoices, cancellation and refund handling, privacy, terms, data export, data deletion, support, and commercial monitoring.

These capabilities are required future destinations but remain separately gated.

### 3.7 API interoperability

The product must be prepared to integrate with other applications and services through governed APIs.

API architecture must preserve versioned contracts, authentication, authorization scopes, auditability, rate limiting, idempotency where relevant, pagination, stable error contracts, retry and timeout policies, secrets management, data minimization, revocation, deprecation policy, sandbox/testing environments, and separation between the core engine and external adapters.

External systems must not connect directly to internal progression logic or unstable internal state.
