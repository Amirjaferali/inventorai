## 2. Purpose and boundary

This document plans how candidates for the two human D13 governance roles (qualified technical expert; independent reviewer) could later be discovered, preliminarily screened against owner-approved criteria using minimal public information, shortlisted, and — under separate owner authorizations — contacted and taken into the canonical evidence and appointment process defined by the Appointment Standard. It also plans the administrative identification of the executing-agent session (§7), which under the current framework involves no human candidate search, and defines the controls that will bind any future administrative screening party (§6).

**This document governs planning and upstream identification only.** It does not and cannot authorize: actual candidate discovery, searching, screening, assessment, ranking, shortlisting, outreach, contact, selection, proposal, or appointment; collection, request, receipt, storage, inspection, or verification of any evidence; Gate 3 issuance; Gate 3A activation; research; architecture; RED; implementation; or Workstream 8. Every such activity remains separately owner-gated. Approval of this document authorizes none of them.

These are internal governance roles, never user-facing referrals; nothing here alters the Technology-First output model or the no-named-provider rule.

## 3. Definitions

Each stage below is distinct. A completed earlier stage never implies a later stage.

- **Discovery** — becoming aware, through a permitted source category (§10), that a person may plausibly fit a role. Creates no status.
- **Preliminary public-information screening** — comparing minimal, publicly available professional information against the owner-approved mandatory criteria, without contact and without formal-evidence collection (§11).
- **Identification** — recording a screened candidate, under an opaque reference, as a private shortlist entry within a bounded owner authorization. For this planning process only, an `IDENTIFIED` entry is treated as a candidate recorded under an opaque reference after preliminary scope-fit screening. This planning interpretation does not redefine the canonical `IDENTIFIED` status, whose validity and transition authority remain governed solely by the Appointment Standard. `IDENTIFIED` confers no expectation, no assessment result, no proposal, and no appointment.
- **Authorized outreach** — first contact with a candidate, only under an express owner outreach authorization naming (by opaque reference) the candidates who may be contacted.
- **Private/formal evidence collection** — requesting or receiving any item on the Appointment Standard §3.1 private-evidence list or any formal evidence category (§13). Separately owner-gated; never part of screening.
- **Evidence verification** — evaluation of collected evidence under the Appointment Standard §4 model, recorded only by the owner or the one specifically authorized verifier (OD-4).
- **Proposal / Appointment / Activation** — the owner-only decisions defined by the Appointment Standard §§5–7. Not restated here.
- **Administrative screening party** — the owner-authorized accountable actor or session that may later conduct discovery and preliminary screening (§6).
- **Public preliminary suitability information** and **private or formal appointment evidence** — the two information tiers defined in §12.

## 8. Qualified technical-expert criteria and bounded authority

The governing criteria are Gate 2 §10, unamended: demonstrable competence in low-voltage embedded electronics; sensor-interface design; microcontroller input/output constraints (logic levels, input impedance, ADC basics); datasheet interpretation; measurement and test planning; failure-mode reasoning; technical documentation; and the ability to distinguish engineering fact from assumption. The bounded technical scope is the Gate 2 §2 concept class (single-signal sensor-to-microcontroller interfacing: analog voltage, single-ended digital logic, pulse/frequency; buses, differential, wireless, mains, high-power, and safety-critical applications excluded).

Competency is evaluated through relevant corroborated evidence, not an arbitrary years-of-experience or brand-name threshold (OD-3); no prestige requirement may exclude competent practitioners without reducing real technical risk. Self-assertion alone is insufficient.

**Bounded authority:**

```text
The appointed qualified technical expert is the designated
technical authority only within the owner-approved,
bounded low-voltage D13 scope and subject to the canonical
claim-specific authority hierarchy.
```

Under Gate 2 §6, authority is claim-specific, not role-based: manufacturer documentation is primary for named-component limits; recognized standards are primary for normative requirements; documented test evidence is primary for observed behavior; the expert interprets and adjudicates but must not silently override any of these. The expert does not: certify the product; issue legal or regulatory approval; decide patentability or inventorship; approve commercial viability; decide governance scope; or act beyond the authorized research scope (Gate 2 §10; Standard §10 excluded_scope).

## 11. Preliminary public-information screening rules

After a future owner authorization (provisional label OD-CI-1; Schemas A and G), the administrative screening party may discover and preliminarily screen potential candidates against the owner-approved mandatory criteria **without contacting them, collecting private or formal evidence, proposing them, or appointing them**, and without a separate owner decision per name discovered, per exclusion, per public source viewed, or per administrative screening step.

Screening may use only minimal publicly available professional information necessary for scope-fit screening: public role or technical field; publicly visible experience; public technical publications; documented public projects; public professional profile information.

Screening must not involve: unnecessary copying or retention of personal data; private CVs or certificates; identity documents; personal contact details beyond what is strictly necessary for later authorized outreach; confidential references; written acceptances; conflict declarations; or formal verification evidence.

Public preliminary information is minimized: recorded only to the extent needed to justify a shortlist or exclusion decision; held privately (§14); never entered into public Git with real identities; and disposed of per the owner-approved retention rule when no longer needed.

The owner authorization may include **shortlist size guidance or a cap, where useful to limit privacy exposure, screening burden, or review cost**. No fixed shortlist-size field is required in every case: the owner may approve one candidate, several candidates, a maximum cap, or no fixed number, depending on the role and the available market. Screening output to the owner is bounded: a private shortlist (Schema B), an exclusion summary (Schema C, reasons per §20 — not per-name dossiers), material ambiguities, and the candidates recommended for authorized outreach. Screening produces findings, never any status beyond `IDENTIFIED` under the bounded authorization, and never a proposal.

## 12. Public-information versus formal-evidence distinction

Two information tiers are distinguished at every stage:

**Public preliminary suitability information** — the §11 categories. This information: is not verified evidence; does not support appointment by itself; must be minimized; must not be copied or retained unnecessarily; and must not enter public Git with real identities.

**Private or formal appointment evidence** — everything on the Appointment Standard §3.1 list; the OD-3 competency-evidence categories (qualifications; documented technical experience; relevant projects; technical publications; specialist training; documented technical assessment); private CVs; certificates; identity evidence; confidential references; written acceptances; conflict declarations; confidentiality commitments; and other appointment-verification material. Its collection remains separately owner-gated (provisional label OD-CI-3) and occurs only privately.

Public preliminary information never converts into formal evidence by use, repetition, or recording; formal evidence status arises only through the canonical §4 evidence process.

## 17. Conflict-of-interest process

Conflict declarations are private/formal evidence: requested only under the evidence-collection authorization, never during screening. Required disclosure at that stage covers: material conflicts; commercial conflicts; provider relationships; prior authorship of candidate content; prior implementation involvement; personal or financial interest; and reviewer-independence conflicts. Screening may record a publicly known potential conflict (for example, a candidate authored candidate technical content) as a §20 reason or an ambiguity for the owner. An unresolved conflict is a stop condition (§25) and, for a reviewer, an independence failure (Standard §17). Recusal and replacement follow the owner decision path and §22. An owner-known or previously used contact receives no independence presumption (§10).

## 19. Neutral candidate-comparison framework

Where more than one candidate passes screening or verification for a role, comparison for the owner proceeds in two strictly separated layers:

**Layer 1 — mandatory pass/fail gates (controlling):** evidence completeness; scope fit; technical competence against Gate 2 §10/§11; independence (for the reviewer); availability; conflict position; confidentiality readiness; verification status. A candidate failing a mandatory gate is not advanced by any comparison result.

**Layer 2 — advisory comparison (subordinate):** the owner may approve a **descriptive or weighted advisory comparison** of gate-passing candidates, only under all of the following conditions: the mandatory pass/fail gates remain separate and controlling; evidence quality and missing evidence remain visible in the comparison output; source familiarity and institutional affiliation carry no automatic weight; the result never automatically proposes or appoints a candidate; the owner retains sole appointment authority; and no score is treated as evidence of competence. A no-scoring, purely descriptive approach remains acceptable where the owner prefers it, but is not mandated as the only safe model. Advisory comparison is a governance-administrative aid only — never product behavior, never product scoring, and never evidence.

## 21. Withdrawal ambiguity and canonical-deference treatment

The Appointment Standard defines `WITHDRAWN` only as a post-`APPOINTED` transition (§5.1), owner-recorded (§7), and does not define `DECLINED` as a candidate withdrawal. **No canonical mechanism currently exists for a candidate's withdrawal of interest before appointment.** This document does not solve that ambiguity by inventing a transition or redefining a status. Instead:

- withdrawal of interest before appointment is recorded as the neutral decision reason `candidate withdrew interest` (§20), with the candidate not advanced further under the current authorization;
- the correct canonical status handling of pre-appointment withdrawal — including whether any recorded status applies and under what authority — is an **unresolved owner clarification point**. This remains an unresolved owner clarification point and must be resolved through an express owner decision or a future canonical amendment before the ambiguity affects an actual case;
- until clarified, any status effect is deferred entirely to the Appointment Standard §§5, 7, and a `DECLINED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `REPLACED`, or `ENDED` role returns to the identification path only through a new bounded owner authorization (§5.2).

## 25. Stop conditions

The administrative screening party (and any party acting under this process) stops and reports to the owner, without further action, when:

1. any activity would exceed the currently authorized stage or boundary;
2. personal or identifying candidate data appears in, or is about to enter, public Git or any committable artifact;
3. **unsolicited evidence:** a candidate sends private evidence before formal evidence collection is authorized — stop processing; secure or quarantine the material within the approved private boundary, performing only the minimum handling necessary for secure receipt and basic classification; do not submit it to any AI service; do not place it in Git; notify the owner; and obtain a handling decision before any further processing;
4. screening cannot proceed without private information;
5. a conflict, independence question, identity doubt, or suspected misrepresentation arises;
