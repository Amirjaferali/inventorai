## PURPOSE OF THIS PAPER

Before any Stage 3 gap taxonomy is proposed, one foundational question must be resolved:

Are the three gap types identified in SA-001A §6 canonical foundations or illustrative examples?

The answer determines taxonomy scope, growth rules, and long-term governance burden. A taxonomy built on the wrong answer will either constrain Stage 3 unnecessarily or permit inflation that undermines the epistemic boundary established in STAGE3_PURPOSE_CLARIFICATION.

No gap types are proposed here. No taxonomy is designed. This paper resolves the philosophy that must govern taxonomy design before it begins.

---

## 1. WHAT EVIDENCE IN SA-001A SUPPORTS EITHER INTERPRETATION?

### 1.1 Evidence for Canonical Foundation Interpretation

SA-001A §6 states:

"What Stage 3 must accomplish that Stage 2 does not:
1. Problem-Mechanism Fit -- does the mechanism address the intended problem?
2. Assumption Inventory -- which underlying assumptions are unvalidated?
3. Expertise-Gap Awareness -- what domain expertise does the inventor lack?"

The phrasing "must accomplish" is prescriptive, not illustrative. SA-001A does not say "may include" or "for example." It says these are what Stage 3 must accomplish. This language supports a canonical interpretation.

Furthermore, SA-001A §6 links these three directly to the Stage 3 exit characterization from SPV §6 Layer 3: inventor identifies prioritized next action, names remaining unknowns, defines proof-of-concept objective, articulates uncertainty reduction. Each of the three gap types maps directly to one component of this exit characterization:

- Problem-Mechanism Fit produces: ability to identify whether the mechanism addresses the intended problem
- Assumption Inventory produces: ability to name remaining unknowns with precision
- Expertise-Gap Awareness produces: ability to articulate what expertise is needed to reduce uncertainty

The structural alignment between the three gap types and the exit characterization suggests these are not arbitrary examples -- they were selected because they jointly produce the epistemic state Stage 3 is designed to develop.

### 1.2 Evidence for Illustrative Interpretation

SA-001A §6 also states: "Gap types at Stage 3: Not yet defined. Confirmed: Problem-Mechanism Fit, Assumption Inventory, Expertise-Gap Awareness. Complete set requires Stage 3 design authorization."

The phrase "complete set requires Stage 3 design authorization" implies the three are a confirmed subset, not a confirmed complete set. This supports an illustrative interpretation -- these three are confirmed members of the taxonomy, but the taxonomy is not closed.

### 1.3 Resolution

The two phrasings are not contradictory. They resolve as follows:

The three gap types are **canonical foundation members** -- their inclusion is confirmed and not subject to design-phase reconsideration. They are what Stage 3 must accomplish. They are not illustrative.

However, the taxonomy is **not closed** -- additional gap types may be proposed if justified by evidence. The foundation is canonical; the ceiling is not fixed.

This resolution matters: it means taxonomy design begins with three confirmed gap types and asks whether additions are justified -- not whether the three are justified.

## 2. IF FOUNDATIONAL, WHAT CRITERIA JUSTIFY ADDITIONAL STAGE 3 GAP TYPES?

Given the canonical foundation interpretation, additional gap types may be proposed only when all of the following criteria are satisfied:

**Criterion 1 -- Epistemic novelty.**
The proposed gap type must assess a dimension of inventor capability that none of the three canonical types already assess. A gap type that measures a variant of assumption awareness is not a new gap type -- it is a question variant within Assumption Inventory.

**Criterion 2 -- Stage 3 specificity.**
The proposed gap type must be genuinely Stage 3-specific. It must not belong to Stage 2 (excluded by GD-001), Stage 1 (situational orientation), or Stage 4+ (engineering readiness or beyond). SA-001A §6 confirms Problem-Mechanism Fit and Assumption Inventory belong to both Stage 1 (surfacing) and Stage 3 (reassessment) -- but they are Stage 3 gap types because Stage 3 reassesses them with implementation orientation. A new gap type must be similarly justified for Stage 3 placement.

**Criterion 3 -- Exit characterization contribution.**
The proposed gap type must contribute to the SA-001A §6 Stage 3 exit characterization. If it does not help the inventor reach the state where they can identify prioritized next action, name remaining unknowns, define proof-of-concept objective, or articulate uncertainty reduction, it does not belong in Stage 3.

**Criterion 4 -- Evidence basis.**
The proposed gap type must be supported by evidence that inventors systematically lack this capability at Stage 2 exit. A gap type proposed without evidence of the gap it addresses is taxonomy inflation.

**Criterion 5 -- Owner authorization.**
Per STAGE3_DESIGN_AUTHORIZATION §2, gap taxonomy proposals require owner review. No gap type beyond the three canonical members may be admitted without explicit owner decision.

## 3. IF ILLUSTRATIVE, WHAT PREVENTS TAXONOMY INFLATION?

This question is partially moot given the canonical foundation resolution in §1. However, the inflation risk remains real regardless of interpretation -- the taxonomy is not closed, and future proposals could expand it without discipline.

Three governance constraints prevent inflation:

**Constraint 1 -- The epistemic boundary test from STAGE_EVOLUTION_POSITION Position 1.**
Any proposed gap type must represent a qualitatively different dimension of inventor capability, not a more granular version of an existing one. Granularity is a question design concern, not a gap type concern.

**Constraint 2 -- The substitution prohibition from SPV §2 Principle 2.**
A gap type that the platform can assess without inventor demonstration -- by providing a structure for the inventor to fill in rather than requiring original reasoning -- is substituting platform capability for inventor capability. Such gap types inflate the taxonomy without developing the inventor.

**Constraint 3 -- The evidence requirement from SR-001 §4.**
A gap type is justified only if it produces evidence of a dimension of inventor improvement defined in SR-001 §3. A gap type that produces no new form of evidence is a protocol addition, not a development addition.

## 4. SHOULD STAGE 3 PURSUE A MINIMAL OR COMPREHENSIVE TAXONOMY?

### 4.1 The Case for Minimal Taxonomy

Stage 2 operates with three gap types and has proven sufficient for its purpose. The three Stage 3 canonical gap types directly map to the Stage 3 exit characterization. A taxonomy that covers the exit characterization completely with the minimum number of gap types is architecturally clean, easier to govern, and harder to inflate.

The history of Stage 2 supports this: GD-001 froze the Stage 2 taxonomy at three gap types precisely because the temptation to add gap types without epistemic justification is real and recurring. A minimal taxonomy with explicit growth criteria is more defensible than a comprehensive taxonomy assembled in advance.

**The minimal approach:** Begin with three canonical gap types. Expand only when Criterion 1-5 in §2 are all satisfied. Treat the exit characterization as the ceiling -- if the three canonical types fully produce it, no expansion is justified.

### 4.2 The Case for Comprehensive Taxonomy

A comprehensive taxonomy designed upfront provides more complete coverage of the Stage 3 epistemic boundary. It reduces the risk of discovering missing gap types after implementation has begun. It allows questions to be designed holistically rather than incrementally.

The counterargument from Stage 2 history: the Stage 2 taxonomy was not designed comprehensively upfront -- it was frozen at three gap types, and that constraint has proven productive rather than limiting. A comprehensive upfront taxonomy risks encoding assumptions about what inventors need before evidence from live sessions has validated those assumptions.

### 4.3 Resolution -- Minimal Foundation with Governed Expansion

The evidence from SA-001A, Stage 2 history, and the governance constraints established in STAGE3_PURPOSE_CLARIFICATION supports a clear position:

**Stage 3 should be built on a minimal canonical foundation that expands only when justified by evidence.**

Rationale:
- The three canonical gap types are already confirmed as sufficient to produce the Stage 3 exit characterization per SA-001A §6
- Adding gap types before evidence of their necessity is taxonomy inflation by definition
- STAGE3_PURPOSE_CLARIFICATION §3 establishes that stage inflation is prevented by requiring epistemic novelty -- a criterion that cannot be satisfied by anticipation, only by evidence
- The governing principle requires implementation readiness to emerge from inventor capability growth -- a minimal taxonomy that develops genuine capability is more aligned with this principle than a comprehensive taxonomy that covers more surface area

Expansion is not prohibited. It is governed. The five criteria in §2 define the expansion conditions.

## 5. WHAT GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLES SHOULD CONSTRAIN FUTURE TAXONOMY GROWTH?

Five principles, derived from committed governance artifacts:

**Principle 1 -- Purpose precedes structure.**
STAGE3_PURPOSE_CLARIFICATION establishes: no gap taxonomy should be designed that cannot be traced back to the purposes defined in that paper. Each gap type must have a traceable purpose justification.

**Principle 2 -- Epistemic novelty required.**
From STAGE_EVOLUTION_POSITION Position 1: a gap type that does not represent a qualitatively new dimension of inventor capability does not belong in the taxonomy regardless of its apparent usefulness.

**Principle 3 -- Exit characterization is the ceiling.**
The Stage 3 exit characterization in SA-001A §6 defines the epistemic state Stage 3 must produce. Gap types that do not contribute to this state have no governance basis. Gap types that exceed it belong to a later stage.

**Principle 4 -- Evidence before expansion.**
From SR-001 §4 Evidence First principle: taxonomy expansion requires evidence that the gap exists systematically in inventors at Stage 2 exit. Anticipated gaps are not governance-authorized gaps.

**Principle 5 -- Owner authorization for every addition.**
No gap type beyond the three canonical members may be added without explicit owner decision. This prevents incremental inflation through well-intentioned but ungoverned additions.

---

## 6. STRATEGIC QUESTION RESOLUTION

**Should Stage 3 be built around a small canonical foundation that expands only when justified by evidence, or should it be designed as a comprehensive taxonomy from the beginning?**

**Answer: Minimal canonical foundation with governed expansion.**

This is not a conservative choice -- it is the epistemically correct choice given the evidence. The three canonical gap types in SA-001A §6 are confirmed, purposeful, and jointly sufficient to produce the Stage 3 exit characterization. Designing a comprehensive taxonomy before evidence of its necessity would substitute anticipation for evidence -- a violation of the project's Evidence First governance principle.

The taxonomy proposal that follows this analysis should begin with the three canonical gap types, define them precisely, establish their relationships and ordering, and leave expansion to the governed process defined in §2 and §5.
